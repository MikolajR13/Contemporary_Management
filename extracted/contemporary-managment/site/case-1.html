Case 1: Technical. Critical bug report to hotfix in production

Scope: starts when a customer reports a bug, ends when the customer is told it is fixed.

Terms: P1 = service unusable for many users, fix immediately; P2 = important but workaround exists. QA = tester who verifies the fix. DevOps = person who deploys to production. Rollback = going back to the previous version.

Actors: Customer, Support, Developer, QA, DevOps.

Facts:

  • Support triages the report and decides P1 or P2.
  • A developer must reproduce the bug first; sometimes it cannot be reproduced and Support asks the customer for more details.
  • Fix, code review, QA test. A failed test returns the fix to the developer.
  • DevOps deploys. If monitoring shows errors after deployment, DevOps rolls back to the previous version, and the bug returns to the developer for another fix.
  • Support informs the customer at the end.

Think about: who waits for whom; which loop is the most expensive.

What to do

  1. Read your case. Underline the start, the end and the actors.
  2. Choose one mapping technique from the lecture (SIPOC, flowchart, swimlane, cross-functional flowchart, value stream map, BPMN) and justify it in two sentences.
  3. Draw the as-is process. Where the case does not say, make an assumption and label it on the map ("assumption: ...").
  4. If you choose a value stream map (or any technique with time), write an estimated time on every step (processing time and wait) and label estimates as assumptions.
  5. Mark one hypothesised bottleneck (where you think work waits or fails) and say why, using facts or labelled assumptions from your map.
  6. Propose one improvement and say which step or handoff changes.

Tools: paper, draw.io, Mermaid, or anything else. Use one notation consistently and add a small legend.
