Case 3: Business. Online store: order to delivery

Scope: starts when the customer places an order, ends when the customer receives the parcel. (Returns are out of scope.)

Actors: Customer, Online shop system, Warehouse, Courier, Finance.

Facts and example data (use or replace with labelled assumptions):

StepProcessing timeWait before the step
Payment check (automatic)2 min0
Order released to warehouse1 min3 h (batched twice a day)
Pick and pack25 min4 h (queue)
Handover to courier5 min16 h (courier collects once a day)
Delivery24 h in transit (elapsed time, no one works on it)0
  • If payment fails, the customer is asked to pay again; after 24 h the order is cancelled.
  • If an item is out of stock, the warehouse asks the customer to wait or cancel.

Think about: what the customer values; what share of the total elapsed time is waiting, and how the 24 h of transit (elapsed, not labour) should be counted.

What to do

  1. Read your case. Underline the start, the end and the actors.
  2. Choose one mapping technique from the lecture (SIPOC, flowchart, swimlane, cross-functional flowchart, value stream map, BPMN) and justify it in two sentences.
  3. Draw the as-is process. Where the case does not say, make an assumption and label it on the map ("assumption: ...").
  4. If you choose a value stream map (or any technique with time), write an estimated time on every step (processing time and wait) and label estimates as assumptions.
  5. Mark one hypothesised bottleneck (where you think work waits or fails) and say why, using facts or labelled assumptions from your map.
  6. Propose one improvement and say which step or handoff changes.

Tools: paper, draw.io, Mermaid, or anything else. Use one notation consistently and add a small legend.
