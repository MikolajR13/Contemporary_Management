Case 2: HR. Onboarding of a new employee (from signed contract to end of day 1)

Scope: starts when the candidate signs the contract, ends at the end of the first working day. (Training after day 1 is out of scope.)

Actors: HR, IT, Facilities, Line manager, New hire.

Facts:

  • HR confirms the signed contract and opens an onboarding request.
  • IT orders a laptop (delivery usually takes 5 to 10 days) and creates accounts after the laptop is registered.
  • Facilities prepares a desk and an access badge; they need the start date and the team from HR.
  • The line manager prepares a first-week plan and books a welcome meeting.
  • Mandatory training (health and safety, GDPR) is assigned by HR before day 1 and must be completed on day 1.
  • Starting a new hire without a laptop or a badge has happened several times in the past year.

Think about: which handoff is most likely to fail, and which step could start earlier.

What to do

  1. Read your case. Underline the start, the end and the actors.
  2. Choose one mapping technique from the lecture (SIPOC, flowchart, swimlane, cross-functional flowchart, value stream map, BPMN) and justify it in two sentences.
  3. Draw the as-is process. Where the case does not say, make an assumption and label it on the map ("assumption: ...").
  4. If you choose a value stream map (or any technique with time), write an estimated time on every step (processing time and wait) and label estimates as assumptions.
  5. Mark one hypothesised bottleneck (where you think work waits or fails) and say why, using facts or labelled assumptions from your map.
  6. Propose one improvement and say which step or handoff changes.

Tools: paper, draw.io, Mermaid, or anything else. Use one notation consistently and add a small legend.
